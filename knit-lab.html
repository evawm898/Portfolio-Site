Knit Lab

Ten wales by ten courses, knitted flat from one yarn: stockinette, stockinette with a row of eyelets across the middle, or 1×1 rib. The yarn is a chain of points; it may not stretch, it resists bending, it may not pass through itself, and where it touches itself it grips with friction. Nothing else tells it how to be a knit. It starts as a rough guess, settles, and is then loaded in small steps, along the wales or across the courses, and let back down.

Load – extension

01What is being simulated

One yarn, about 3,500 points half a yarn diameter apart. Every substep it obeys three rules, solved with extended position-based dynamics (XPBD): consecutive points keep their spacing, each point stays near the midpoint of its neighbours (bending stiffness), and no two non-neighbouring stretches of yarn come closer than one yarn diameter (contact, found through a spatial hash). Where yarn touches yarn it grips with Coulomb friction, μ = 0.25, a dry yarn-on-yarn value. There is no gravity.

Friction here is static: a contact that is not pushed past its Coulomb limit does not slide at all. That needs care in a solver like this one, because the yarn’s length is solved last in each substep and slides the yarn along every contact it passes; each contact therefore remembers the slip it let through and takes it back on the next substep. Without that memory every stuck contact crept by a little each substep, at a rate set by the time step, so how much friction the test showed depended on how fast it pulled — and a swatch held still in the jam slowly tightened itself without end.

The yarn’s length is solved as one linear system along the whole chain each substep (it is tridiagonal, so that costs no more than a sweep), so tension reaches from jaw to jaw at once: the yarn stays within a few hundredths of a percent of its length right up to the load limit. The simulation runs in a Web Worker, so the page keeps drawing while it settles.

The knit structure is not a constraint. It lives only in the starting geometry: a rough guess where every new loop’s legs pass in front of the head of the loop it was pulled through, and its feet pass behind that loop’s legs. If the contact rule holds, the yarn can never undo that interlocking, so whatever it settles into is still a knit. A certificate checks that on every substep: no stretch of yarn moves far enough to have crossed another.

02How it is held

The cast-on course is clamped in a jaw. The live stitches of the last course hang on a comb, one pin through each loop where the next course’s legs would be, and the yarn’s end is tied to it: a frictionless knit with a free end and live stitches has no resting state at all, it unravels. The swatch lies between two frictionless plates a few yarn diameters apart, or its free side edges roll up without end, the way stockinette curls.

The swatch settles without friction, the way a knit is relaxed wet, so every test starts from the same swatch whatever its friction. While settling, the comb follows the load down until the swatch carries a small tension, the preload a tensile tester uses to define the gauge length.

The test is quasi-static. The driven jaw moves one step, 1% of the gauge, slowly, and stops; the swatch is then held until both jaws read the same load and that load has stopped changing, and only then is the point recorded. Step by step it goes up to a set load, several times what it carries at the jam, and back down to where it started. The work lost between the two curves is the hysteresis; how much further out the way back runs at the same small load is the permanent set.

Across the courses the cast-on and the comb would hold the width, so they are first released: the cast-on slides freely along its rail, the comb’s pins slide along it like stitches on a needle, and the swatch settles again at its own width. The two edge wales are then gripped in side jaws, the left one held still to read the load and the right one driven outward.

03The topology record

Each loop records the loops of the previous course it passes through: one for a knit or a purl stitch, none for a yarn over or the cast-on, two for a k2tog or ssk, in the order they lie. The solver never reads it; only the starting-geometry builder does, so the eyelet rows (yarn over and k2tog, or ssk and yarn over, repeated) and 1×1 rib are that record plus one case each in the builder. A purl loop is pulled through from the front instead of the back, so it is drawn mirrored in depth; for a k2tog the right-hand parent’s head is drawn over the left one’s, for an ssk the left over the right, so the new loop’s legs pass through both; a yarn over passes through nothing and stands in the gap the decrease left. The drawn start is checked by linking numbers: each leg of each loop must pass once through exactly the loops it was charted through, from the back for a knit leg and from the front for a purl one, and each decrease must keep the parent it lists first on top.

04What this is, and isn’t

A feasibility spike, not a finished field note. Forces are in model units, not newtons; the yarn has no twist, no compressible cross-section and no material data, and its friction coefficient is a plausible number, not a measured one. Even without friction the swatch does not come back along the way it went out: the knit, lying between the plates, can rest in more than one shape at the same extension, and it settles into a different one on the way back. What it is for is to find out whether a browser can relax, load and unload a yarn-level knit, and whether the result behaves like a knit.
