Knit Lab

Ten wales by ten courses of stockinette, knitted flat from one yarn. The yarn is a chain of points; it may not stretch, it resists bending, and it may not pass through itself. Nothing else tells it how to be a knit. It starts as a rough guess, settles, and is then pulled from the top.

Run

After it settles: drag anywhere above the top course to pull by hand, or press Pull for a slow constant-speed pull that logs force against extension. It stops early if the yarn itself starts to stretch.

Load – extension

Reaction on the bottom jaw (model units) against jaw-to-jaw extension.

01What is being simulated

One yarn, about 3,500 points half a yarn diameter apart. Every substep it obeys three rules, solved with extended position-based dynamics (XPBD): consecutive points keep their spacing (the yarn is near-inextensible), each point stays near the midpoint of its neighbours (bending stiffness), and no two non-neighbouring stretches of yarn come closer than one yarn diameter (contact, found through a spatial hash). There is no friction and no gravity.

The knit structure is not a constraint. It lives only in the starting geometry: a rough guess where every new loop’s legs pass in front of the head of the loop it was pulled through, and its feet pass behind that loop’s legs. If the contact rule holds, the yarn can never undo that interlocking, so whatever it settles into is still a knit. A certificate checks that on every substep: no stretch of yarn moves far enough to have crossed another.

02How it is held

The cast-on course is clamped in a jaw. The live stitches of the last course hang on a comb, one pin through each loop where the next course’s legs would be, and the yarn’s end is tied to it: a frictionless knit with a free end and live stitches has no resting state at all, it unravels. The swatch lies between two frictionless plates a few yarn diameters apart, or its free side edges roll up without end, the way stockinette curls.

While settling, the comb follows the load down until the swatch carries a small tension, the preload a tensile tester uses to define the gauge length. Pulling drives the comb up at constant speed and logs the force on the bottom jaw.

03The topology record

Each loop records the loops of the previous course it passes through: one for a plain knit stitch, none for a yarn over or the cast-on, two for a k2tog or ssk, in the order they lie. The solver never reads it; only the starting-geometry builder does, so a yarn over or a decrease is a change to that record and one case in the builder, not to the simulation.

04What this is, and isn’t

A feasibility spike, not a finished field note. Forces are in model units, not newtons; the yarn has no friction, no twist, no compressible cross-section and no material data. Past the point where the loops jam, the pull is carried by the yarn itself, and one solver sweep per substep cannot keep it inextensible under that load, so the automatic pull stops when any piece of yarn is strained 5%. What it is for is to find out whether a browser can relax and pull a yarn-level knit at interactive speed, and whether the result behaves like a knit.
