E-Ink Dress Shell

Placing rigid display panels on a rigid 3D garment — a corseted bodice and bell skirt formed in three dimensions from the start and never flattened. The tool answers the governing question directly: what fits where?

01Premise

Unlike the earlier flat-pattern skirt study, this shell is doubly curved, so it has no flat development at all. Every question has to be answered on the surface itself. The current geometry is the confirmed skirt: waist 24″, hem 61″, 15″ drop, with a superellipse profile setting the perimeter of every level and each level solved as an ellipse at a constant 1.5 : 1 ratio — the bodice’s waist ratio carried all the way to the hem (586 mm wide × 391 mm deep), so the skirt meets the future bodice edge-to-edge at the waist seam. The elliptical hem is a deliberate trade: flattening center front is what makes room for the largest panel (below). The waist itself is no longer a crease but a solved fillet: a 25 mm conic blend joins skirt and bodice tangentially, constructed against a slightly smaller virtual waist so the blend’s closest approach still measures exactly the given 24″ (the minimum ring migrates ~9 mm up into the bodice). The fillet region doubles as the seam band and cable bus — the keep-out is derived from the blend’s actual extent rather than authored — panel footprints must clear it, and connector tails terminate at its edge. A body-centered coordinate system covers the surface — azimuth θ from center front (equal-arc, so a degree of θ is the same number of millimeters anywhere on a ring), arc length s from the waist, negative upward into the bodice — and a snap grid ruled in those coordinates stays honest on the curved surface.

Above the fillet rises the bodice, and its depth is no longer guessed: the designer traced a reference garment in true side view, the trace was measured per-scanline and fitted (residuals under ~1 mm), and the bodice’s BACK depth follows that traced shape exactly — normalized to the given waist so the pieces still meet edge-to-edge at the seam. The FRONT depth no longer matches it: each level is a compound section, two half-ellipses sharing one half-width, and the front half carries its own authored bust cup — nearly straight runs from the waist to a maximum-projection point 22 mm below the bust apex, then back in to the neckline, with a blended 30 mm-radius corner at the peak and a sharp corner where it leaves the back profile. The circumference schedule is frozen through this change — the half-width is re-solved so the compound perimeter still matches the given waist/underbust/bust exactly — so the dress narrows in front view everywhere the front deepens (7–16 mm at the anchors) while the tape measurements hold. The cross-section ratio at the bust remains a reported output (about 1.65), now describing an asymmetric section, not an ellipse. The neckline is the physical top edge, now the asymmetric-in-height one-shoulder line the design calls for: center front at 220 mm rises to a genuine corner peak of 240 mm at 82°, then falls steeply — two-thirds of the 95 mm total drop happens in the first 30° past the peak — through the sides (190 mm) to a low back (145 mm), easing to a zero tangent only in the last 15° before center back. The shell is cut at the curve, panels must stay 6 mm below it for the edge finish, and each segment is asserted monotone so the curve cannot kink. The garment’s front/back seam is not at the sides: it sits at the solved armhole angle, ±103°, found by wrapping the 360 mm across-back tape along the actual surface at 190 mm height — a straight-chord estimate would miss by ~17 mm — and an 8 mm keep-out band flanks the seam.

For every grid cell the tool computes principal curvatures and, more usefully, the max seatable panel class: the largest rigid panel from the library that can sit tangent there without lifting more than 2 mm off the surface. Panels are then authored on one side only, at any rotation — placement rotation is free; mirror twins are derived, never stored. A physical panel cannot be reflected, so each twin keeps the source’s rotation or adds 180°, chosen by connector legality, then by closeness to true reflection.

03What the Analysis Says

Numbers below are read from the committed analysis file (the same data driving the shading), not recomputed by this page.

loading dress/dress-analysis.json …

The panel library is real hardware — three Adafruit bare e-ink modules (2.13″ quad-color, 3.7″ and 7.5″ tri-color), dimensioned from their manufacturer datasheets. Four findings shaped the layout. First, the 7.5″ is not a placeable panel on this garment at all: at 111 mm across it needs roughly a 770 mm local radius to stay under the 2 mm standoff tolerance, and no cell comes close. It is seated instead on a deliberate flat facet on the skirt — the shell is locally flattened across the panel footprint (with a smooth blend band), and the silhouette cost is reported as the shell’s deviation, ochre-flagged in the viewer. Second, the 3.7″ conforms nowhere on this dress at the 2 mm tolerance — the traced bodice curves just past its limit (best seat ~2.3 mm), and the sweep shows it returning across 13% of cells if the tolerance is relaxed to 2.5 mm. Third, the bust cup costs most of the center-front column: the compound section’s own corner at the maximum-projection point holds a curvature keep-out roughly 60–70 mm tall regardless of how the corner is blended (a 30 mm blend radius softens the break but widens the band it opens, not the other way around), and a second, unblended corner where the front leaves the back profile costs another band lower down — together they cut the seatable run at center front from a continuous 170 mm to roughly 90 mm split across two islands. Fourth, near ±90°–110° the section curves tightest and even the 2.13″ cannot seat — but with the seam moved back to the solved armhole angle (±103°), that unseatable ring now falls partly mid-front-piece rather than along the physical seam, an honest cost of the one-shoulder construction the report calls out. The 2 mm tolerance itself is an untested assumption; the committed analysis carries a sweep (1.5–3 mm) showing how the map shifts if the real number differs.

04Pipeline

Everything lives in the repo at tools/dress-shell/, outside the site build: the parametric shell and exact coordinate maps (round-tripping at machine precision), the panel library and layout source-of-truth in YAML with lossless canonical saves, the mirroring/derivation engine, per-cell curvature and seating analysis, a layering engine (mount-height stacking, occlusion, connector-escape checks), and a local three.js placement editor with a small dev server — click to place on the snapped grid, drag to re-seat, undo/redo, save, and an explicit publish step that writes the static files this page loads. The design itself is a committed YAML file; this page only ever changes when a new layout is deliberately published.
