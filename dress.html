E-Ink Dress Shell

Placing rigid display panels on a rigid 3D garment — a corseted bodice and bell skirt formed in three dimensions from the start and never flattened. The tool answers the governing question directly: what fits where?

01Premise

Unlike the earlier flat-pattern skirt study, this shell is doubly curved, so it has no flat development at all. Every question has to be answered on the surface itself. The current geometry is the confirmed skirt: waist 24″, hem 61″, 15″ drop, with a superellipse profile setting the perimeter of every level and each level solved as an ellipse at a constant 1.5 : 1 ratio — the bodice’s waist ratio carried all the way to the hem (586 mm wide × 391 mm deep), so the skirt meets the future bodice edge-to-edge at the waist seam. The elliptical hem is a deliberate trade: flattening center front is what makes room for the largest panel (below). The waist itself is a crease, and an ±8 mm seam band around it is reserved as the cable bus: panel footprints must clear it, and connector tails terminate at its edge. A body-centered coordinate system covers the surface — azimuth θ from center front (equal-arc, so a degree of θ is the same number of millimeters anywhere on a ring), arc length s from the waist, negative upward into the bodice — and a snap grid ruled in those coordinates stays honest on the curved surface.

Above the crease rises the bodice, and its depth is no longer guessed: the designer traced a reference garment in true side view, the trace was measured per-scanline and fitted (residuals under ~1 mm), and the bodice’s front-to-back depth follows that traced shape — normalized to the given waist so the pieces still meet edge-to-edge at the seam — rising monotonically to a smooth crest at the bust and tapering above. The side-to-side width is then solved from the given circumferences (waist 24″, underbust 28″, bust 34″) via Ramanujan’s perimeter formula, so the cross-section ratio is a reported output (about 1.64 at the bust), never an assumption. The neckline is the physical top edge — a curve with a zero-tangent apex at center front (250 mm above the waist), a shoulder plateau near 50°, a smooth arrival at the sides (205 mm), and a flat run around the back; the shell is cut at the curve, panels must stay 6 mm below it for the edge finish, and the whole curve is asserted monotone so mirroring cannot kink it.

For every grid cell the tool computes principal curvatures and, more usefully, the max seatable panel class: the largest rigid panel from the library that can sit tangent there without lifting more than 2 mm off the surface. Panels are then authored on one side only; mirror twins are derived, never stored — a physical panel cannot be reflected, so each twin picks identity or 180° rotation by connector legality, then by closeness to true reflection.

03What the Analysis Says

The panel library is real hardware — three Adafruit bare e-ink modules (2.13″ quad-color, 3.7″ and 7.5″ tri-color), dimensioned from their manufacturer datasheets. Four findings shaped the layout. First, the 7.5″ is not a placeable panel on this garment at all: at 111 mm across it needs roughly a 770 mm local radius to stay under the 2 mm standoff tolerance, and no cell comes close. It is seated instead on a deliberate flat facet on the skirt — the shell is locally flattened across the panel footprint (with a smooth blend band), and the silhouette cost is reported as the shell’s deviation, ochre-flagged in the viewer. Second, the 3.7″ conforms nowhere on this dress at the 2 mm tolerance — the traced bodice curves just past its limit (best seat ~2.3 mm), and the sweep shows it returning across 13% of cells if the tolerance is relaxed to 2.5 mm. Third, the bust crest is panel-free by physics: the meridian’s bend at the apex concentrates curvature, so every 2.13″ centered between the bust and the neckline would lift 5–8 mm off the surface — the highest seatable 2.13″ tops out about 35 mm below the apex, and the band above stays bare shell (the above-bust taper costs nothing: the comparison against a constant section showed 0% seatable either way). Fourth, near the ±90° side seams even the 2.13″ cannot seat — widest at the waist, exactly where the garment’s physical seams are. The 2 mm tolerance itself is an untested assumption; the committed analysis carries a sweep (1.5–3 mm) showing how the map shifts if the real number differs.

04Pipeline

Everything lives in the repo at tools/dress-shell/, outside the site build: the parametric shell and exact coordinate maps (round-tripping at machine precision), the panel library and layout source-of-truth in YAML with lossless canonical saves, the mirroring/derivation engine, per-cell curvature and seating analysis, a layering engine (mount-height stacking, occlusion, connector-escape checks), and a local three.js placement editor with a small dev server — click to place on the snapped grid, drag to re-seat, undo/redo, save, and an explicit publish step that writes the static files this page loads. The design itself is a committed YAML file; this page only ever changes when a new layout is deliberately published.
