Quantifying Comfort

— Notes from a Hohenstein talk on how textile labs put numbers on a feeling.

01The problem

“Comfortable” is subjective. The lab does not try to measure it whole. It splits it into three:

  • Thermo-physiological — heat and moisture.
  • Skin-sensorial — touch.
  • Ergonomic — fit.

The influencing factors run in a chain: fiber → yarn → fabric → finishing → garment and fit → layering.

02The system

Three parts: body, climate, clothing.

Heat gain is metabolic plus whatever the environment adds. It has to balance heat loss, which leaves five ways: conduction, convection, radiation, evaporation, respiration.

The controller is the hypothalamus. The effectors are the sweat glands, skeletal muscle and the skin arterioles.

03What gets measured

Two numbers: Rct, thermal resistance, in m²·K/W, and Ret, water vapor resistance — breathability — in m²·Pa/W.

Both come from the sweating guarded hotplate, the “skin model,” under ISO 11092.

05What I checked

  • Diffusion definition, thermoregulation, heat-balance model, ISO 11092 description: verified accurate.
  • Sweat table: consistent with published ranges (0.5–2.5 L/hr endurance; marathon ~2.5 L median, up to ~6.8 L) but marathon and Ironman run high. Ironman 20 L is gross production over 10–17 h, not net loss (finishers lose ~1–6% bodyweight). No primary source found; illustrative.
  • Core-temp graphic claimed severe damage/death at ±4 °F. Hot side is close (heat stroke ≈ 104 °F). Cold side understated: 94.6 °F is only mild hypothermia; life-threatening is below ~82–92 °F.
