# /frame — charter

The governing document for the Parametric Frame (`frame.html`, `frame.css`, `frame.js`,
`frame-registry.js`, `frame-geometry.js`, `frame-profile-editor.js`). Read it before
touching any frame file. The working agreements of the flower-project skill apply
verbatim (registry as the single source of truth, one owner per boundary, generated by
the shape and never clipped to it, the loudness budget, contact sheets before commits,
numbers not summaries, shipped means reachable); this charter holds what is specific to
the frame and does not restate them.

## 1. What it is

A generator for ornamental frames and Gothic windows as a PRINTABLE RELIEF, with an
engraved, lightly shaded, tattoo-flash drawing of the same frame beside it. The references
are ornate silver mirror frames, oval baroque frames, and Gothic lancet windows with
tracery and ivy.

**Output contract (ruling 1, phase 2 — Eva, Oct 22).** The RELIEF STL is the primary
output and the SVG is the secondary one, the /bug pattern: one model, two readers.
- **STL** (binary, millimetres, `exportStl(relief)` in `frame-relief.js`): the skeleton
  and the molding as a relief — every band's profile `z(x)` lifted over a flat back, the
  lights pierced (default) or backed. Later layers land here first: filigree as relief
  geometry, texture as surface displacement. Hatching is NOT in the STL; in 3D the molding
  shades itself under the one fixed light.
- **SVG** (`exportSvg(model)` in `frame-geometry.js`): layers 1–3, the engraving — the
  swept bands, creases, hatching derived from the profile's normal, cross-hatch. Unchanged
  by phase 2: the default's SVG is byte-identical to `8db018c` (the gate measures it).
- **Both read one model.** `buildFrame` builds the skeleton and the sweep; `buildRelief`
  reads that model and `offsetCurve` and adds nothing to the 2-D geometry; `exportSvg`
  never reads the relief. The 3D view on the page draws the STL's own positions.

**Core model.** Every frame is a PATH (the skeleton) with a MOLDING PROFILE swept along
it. Arch, rectangle and oval are skeleton types feeding the same sweep. The arch is the
open-bottomed / silled case of the one system, never a separate tool.

**Print process is undecided (ruling 3).** Every millimetre floor below is an ASSUMPTION
until a process is chosen and a coupon printed; nothing here has been printed.

## 2. The layer stack

Outer to inner. Settle each before refining the next — a layer is constrained by the one
before it, and ornament tuned against a skeleton that later moves is work done twice.

| # | layer | what it owns | phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Skeleton** | the path: arch (one continuous pointedness; ogee and Tudor later as separate constructions), rectangle (mitre / rounded / notched / eared corners), oval (aspect; the circle is 1:1); width, height, proportion; mullion count, sill; the NAMED ANCHORS ornament grows from | 1 (arch), 2b (rect/oval, anchors) |
| 2 | **Molding** | one 1-D cross-section profile swept along every spine; the draggable profile editor; presets as starting points; width and depth. | 3 | **Shading** | SVG only: hatching derived from the swept surface's normal against one fixed light; intensity, pitch, line weight — the weight FOLLOWS the darkness (1b), and cross-hatching lands only above a threshold (1b). In 3D the molding shades itself under the same `LIGHT` | 1, 1b |

## 3. Invariants

1. **One geometry path.** `buildFrame(params, profile)` is the one builder; the drawing
   on screen is `exportSvg(model)`, the same string the download writes. The gate asserts
   this path for path (X1).
2. **The registry is the source of truth.** `frame-registry.js` declares every control,
   section, default, visibility predicate and profile preset. The page generates the
   panel from it and never names a control by hand. Adding a control is one row.
3. **One owner per boundary** (§4). No consumer adds `d · normal` to a spine except
   `offsetCurve`. The gate scans for a second producer (O4) and refuses it.
4. **The shade comes from the geometry.** Darkness is `intensity × (1 − Lambert)` of the
   profile's own normal against `LIGHT`, a fixed vector in the registry. There is no
   light control, no painted gradient and no per-band tint. A flat profile is EXACTLY
   uniform (T1, Tp1); a cove grades (T2, Tp2). **Line weight follows that same darkness**
   (ruling 1, phase 1b): a hatch run's stroke width is `lineWeight × hatchWeight(d)`, one
   law in `frame-geometry.js` reading `HATCH_WEIGHT_RANGE` / `HATCH_WEIGHT_STEPS` from the
   registry, so a flat band is one weight and a graded band several (W1, W2). **Cross-hatch
   is a threshold on the same darkness** (ruling 6): lines across the band only where it
   exceeds `crossHatchThreshold` — none at 1, every band at 0, a band IFF its own darkness
   exceeds it (H1) — and the threshold is an ADVANCED-tier control (U3).
5. **Topology is mode-free and a ladder is derived from a length.** Row counts, sample
   counts and line counts are derived from millimetres (band width over hatch pitch,
   arc step in degrees), never typed as counts.
6. **Clamped and told.** A rise taller than the height, a light too narrow for its
   sub-arch — the geometry holds the value at its floor and `info.clamped` names it; the
   read-out prints it (C1). Never silently stretched, never refused.
7. **Nothing is clipped to the outline.** Hatch lines are generated as offsets of the
   spine they belong to and run out where the tone falls under their threshold; they are
   never drawn across the band and trimmed.
8. **The light and the paper are fixed.** Ink `#0A0A0C` on paper `#EDEDE8`; the page's
   chrome is the site's dark palette around it.
9. **The export is ONE connected watertight solid** (phase 2, from the bloom's own
   invariant). The hard gate is boundary edges = 0 on the welded float32 bytes the STL
   writer wrote (W). Connectedness is VOLUMETRIC: every triangle rasterised into a voxel
   grid at 0.4 mm, 6-connected flood fill, one region; a miss is re-read at 0.2 mm, because
   a real gap survives a smaller cell and a sampling artefact vanishes (C). Both are the
   bug gate's own code, ported. Two dead ends are recorded so nobody re-derives them: a
   vertex-weld shell count is not connectedness (the solid is many closed shells that
   overlap without sharing vertices), and an AABB-overlap graph is worse than no gate
   (boxes overlap when the solids inside them do not). **Overlapping closed shells are
   accepted** — at the apex mitre, at the sub-arch joins, and at every band end, where the
   relief overlaps its neighbour by a declared EMBED (§4) rather than touching it face to
   face. A face-to-face contact reads as one region to the voxel gate and is NOT an
   overlap; the embed is therefore asserted geometrically (B), not by the flood fill.
10. **`MIN_FEATURE_MM` is one constant (1.0 mm, in the registry), labelled an ASSUMPTION
   wherever it is quoted** — here, in the read-out, in the gate — pending the print
   process (ruling 3). It caps the OUTPUT, never the input: a plate thinner than it is
   built at the floor and told; a scaled band or mullion narrower than it is told, not
   refused (`info.underFloor`, F). It is also the embed length (a length derived from a
   length, never a count).

The gate's O1–O4 measure this table rather than recite it: jambs and mullions start on
`innerEdge(sill)` to the double; the clear widths are equal measured from the jambs'
inner edges to the mullions' outer edges; sub-arches spring at one height — the outer two
on the jambs' inner edges and the interior ones on the centrelines — and clear
`innerEdge(head)` by one mullion band; and `+ d * spine.nrm` appears once in the source.
H2 asks the same question of every cross line: both ends ON an offset polyline of the band.

## 5. The arch, as built

Half-span `a` is between the jamb CENTRELINES (`width / 2`). `R = a (1 + 2p)`: `p` 0 is
the semicircle, 0.5 the equilateral arch (R = span), 1 the lancet (R = 3a). Both centres
sit on the spring line, `R − a` inside the opposite springing, so the apex is on the
axis at every `p` and the jamb-to-head join is tangent-continuous. `height` is the sill's
underside to the apex; the jambs take what the rise leaves.

Lights: `n` mullions make `n + 1` lights of EQUAL CLEAR WIDTH (jamb inner edge to mullion
outer edge, mullion to mullion). Each light carries a sub-arch of the same pointedness on
its own half-span, springing on the main spring line unless its apex would come within
one mullion band of the head's inner edge, when the sub-spring line drops (ruling 3, 1b:
kept). **The outer sub-arches terminate on the jambs' INNER EDGES** (ruling 4, 1b — read
off `innerEdge(jamb)`, the boundary the clear widths already read; phase 1 ran them to the
jamb centreline, half-buried); the interior joins are mullion centreline to centreline, so
a sub-arch's end is half-buried in the mullion band — accepted. Consequence: the outer
lights' sub-arches are narrower by half a mullion band than the interior ones, so their
apexes sit lower (229.3 against 234.0 mm at the defaults).

The sill is a horizontal spine at `w / 2` whose top edge is what everything stands on;
`stepped` adds a wider, thinner spine below it.

## 6. Shading, as built

The band's across-position `x ∈ [0, 1]` (outer edge to inner). The profile's slope
`dz/ds = (depth / width) · dz/dx` tilts the surface normal about the spine's tangent,
so `N = (−dz/ds · n, 1) / |…|` where `n` is the spine's inward 2-D normal. Darkness
`= intensity · (1 − max(0, N · LIGHT))`. Consequences, all measured by the gate: a flat
band is one normal everywhere, so one tone; a vertical jamb on the left reads
differently from the right under the one light (T3); the same profile on the arch head
grades ALONG the arc as its normal turns.

Hatch: `floor(width / pitch)` candidate lines parallel to the spine. Line `k` is drawn
over the vertices where darkness exceeds a van der Corput threshold `vdc(k)` — evenly
spread thresholds, so the line count is proportional to darkness and each line runs out
exactly where the tone drops under its own threshold. Creases (slope turns > 22°) get a
drawn line; the two edges always do.

**Weight (1b, ruling 1).** Every hatch run is drawn at `lineWeight × (0.5 + d)` for its
darkness `d`, quantised onto six levels; a run SPLITS where its level changes (the boundary
vertex is shared) so a line running from shade into light swells and thins along its
length. The darkest line is three times the lightest; the edges stay at 1.4 × nominal.
Default intensity is **1.00** (ruling 1). Measured on the cove's left jamb through the
close camera, outer → inner bins of pixel ink: **0.235 / 0.235 / 0.118** against phase 1's
0.176 / 0.118 / 0.118 at intensity 0.65 — the outer gradient reads as two lines' worth of
ink where it read as one, and the weights on that jamb run 0.30–0.57 mm at the 0.40
nominal. The SVG carries one `<path>` per distinct width per band (W2), so a 9-band frame
is a few dozen paths rather than hundreds.

**Cross-hatch (1b, ruling 6).** Where the darkness exceeds `crossHatchThreshold`
(default **0.70**, Advanced tier), a line is laid ACROSS the band every `hatchPitch` mm
along the spine, covering exactly the profile samples above the threshold — a deep cove,
a shadowed step, a reed's shaded wall. It is a chord between two `offsetCurve` polylines
(H2), never a second producer, and it runs out where the tone drops. At 0.70 the shipped
default cross-hatches 6 of 9 bands (366 lines); the reeded and fluted presets, whose
walls reach darkness 1.00, carry thousands (6,750 / 4,084) — the molding is the loudest
element there, which ruling 2 says it must not be once filigree lands, and the sheet
photographs it for the ruling. Perpendicular rather than at 45°, and at the hatch pitch
rather than a pitch of its own: both are decisions without a ruling (§9).

## 7b. The closed skeletons, as built (phase 2b)

**One spine, closed.** The rectangle and the oval are each ONE spine with `closed: true`,
traversed counter-clockwise with the inward normal on the left (the arch head's sense),
starting at the BOTTOM CENTRE — a point on a straight side, or the oval's bottom vertex —
so the seam sits where the shape is mirror-symmetric and its ANTIPODE (vertex V/2, the top
centre) is its twin. No vertex is duplicated; the relief joins row V−1 to row 0 by a
closing strip of the same quads every other pair of rows gets, and caps nothing.

**Rectangle.** `width` is between the side bands' centrelines (the arch's convention),
`height` the outer box from y = 0. One corner — the bottom-right, travelling +x then +y —
is built and rotated by whole quarter turns onto the other three, so the four are
congruent and every corner's anchor is the vertex on its own bisector: the mitre, the
arc's 45° point, the notch's or the ear's diagonal step. `cornerSize` is the rounded
corner's CENTRELINE radius, or the notch's / ear's step. Clamped and told: a radius whose
inner edge would be under half a feature, or would leave no straight side; a step whose
own inner face would be under a feature (`≥ w + F`), or would meet the next corner.

**Oval.** Semi-axes `B = (height − w)/2` up and `A = aspect · B` across (the width control
is hidden: the aspect is the oval's own proportion, and 1.00 is the circle). Sampled
uniformly in the NORMAL'S ANGLE — the arc's own 1.5° of turning per vertex, generalised —
so the point for normal angle φ is `(A² cos φ, B² sin φ) / √(A² cos² φ + B² sin² φ)` and the
inward normal is exactly `−(cos φ, sin φ)`. The inner edge stays a curve iff the half band
is under the least radius of curvature `min(A,B)²/max(A,B)`; the aspect is clamped so the
inner edge keeps half a feature of radius, and TOLD. 240 vertices.

**Measured.** Every vertex on the skeleton RESTATED from the controls to ≤ 2.2e-13 mm
(K2). The seam: gap 0 and normal discontinuity 0 at the loop start in the plane (Q1); in
the mesh, |turn across the seam − turn across its antipode| ≤ 1.2e-13 rad on every column
of 63 closed rows (Q, `atan2` — an `acos` read 3e-8 of rounding as a step). A seam moved
0.1 mm, on the oval and on the rectangle, is caught by both gates; a closing strip dropped
is caught by W and Q. The opening across the sides in the STL equals `2a − w` / `2A − w`
to 2.8e-14 mm (L2). Triangles at the defaults: rectangle mitre 1,568, rounded 48,608,
notched 3,136, eared 4,704; oval 47,040 (the arch 68,076, unmoved).

**Found, not fixed, recorded:** (a) the ARCH is not mirror-exact at the double — its left
springing is `R·sin π` (~3e-14 mm) above its right and its apex x is ~1e-14; AN holds the
arch to 1e-9 mm and prints the residue, because rebuilding it on `quarterTable` would move
its frozen bytes. (b) Darkness at a MITRE vertex reads the mitre normal, whose length is
`1/cos(turn/2)` (√2 at a rectangle corner, ~1.15 at the arch apex), so the shading's
normal is over-tilted at that one vertex per corner — pre-existing at the apex; normalising
it would move the arch's SVG. (c) The voxel sampler (C) was replaced by rows parallel to
each triangle's longest edge: the n × n grid spent 5–7 s on a rectangle's long thin quads;
the row sampler reads 2% fewer voxels (grazes at cell corners), which can only SPLIT a
region, never merge two — stricter, never blinder (header of `voxelComponents`).

## 8. Verification plan

Per phase, the gate is `node tools/verify-frame.mjs` (part one Node in seconds, part two
Chromium in ~40 s; `--negative-control` runs the mutants, part one only, and says so;
`--no-browser` for iteration) and the sheet is `node tools/shot-frame-sheet.mjs <dir>`.
**The gate runs in CI** (`.github/workflows/frame.yml`, since 1b): the full gate and then
the negative control, on any PR touching the frame files, the two frame tools, the charter
or the workflow. **A frame-only PR no longer runs the flower gates**: the two that carried
`'tools/**'` (`flower-export-watertight`, `flower-geometry-quality`) are narrowed to the
files they reach, proved by a picomatch simulation of every workflow's `paths` over a
frame-only, a cards-only and a flower diff (frame → `frame.yml` alone; flower-geometry.js
→ the five flower workflows; before: both flower gates on every frame and cards diff).

Every phase owes:
- a byte comparison against a worktree of the base commit for the rows the change
  should not move (`exportSvg` strings are the bytes here);
- a clause in the O family for every new boundary, with a mutant that restates it;
- a tone measurement on pixels through a close camera, not the whole-frame view (1.3
  px/mm, where a 0.4 mm line is grey);
- a contact sheet at one pinned viewBox, captions carrying the read-out's numbers, no
  pixel delta quoted (the renderer is deterministic here — V0 reads 0 px twice — but a

### 10.1 Ruling 5: where the inner edge folds, measured

`node tools/frame-fold-boundary.mjs` builds a COPY of `frame-geometry.js` with every clamp
removed, and BISECTS the onset of a fold on the emitted edges — a fold is an edge segment that
runs against its own centreline segment, or an edge that crosses itself — over four band widths
(6 / 18 / 30 / 40 mm). Results, every row:

| corner | fold measured | law | shipped floor | margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rounded | radius = w/2 (to 1e-5) | inner arc radius 0 | (w + F)/2 | F/2 of inner radius |
| oval, narrow | aspect = √((w/2)/B) (to 1e-4) | least radius A²/B = w/2 | √(((w + F)/2)/B) | F/2 of inner radius |
| oval, wide | aspect = B/(w/2) (to 1e-3) | least radius B²/A = w/2 | B/((w + F)/2) | F/2 of inner radius |
| eared | step = w | the inner edge loops back through the ear's own step | w + F | F |
| notched | **never** (swept 0.05 … 2w) | each offset step keeps length sz | **F** (was w + F) | the feature itself |

So phase 2b's rounded, oval and eared floors were right — computed, and now measured. **The
notched floor was not a fold**: a notch's offset steps keep their length at every size (one end
convex, one concave), so `w + F` was a guess. It is the feature, F, now (the slider's own 2 mm
minimum is above it, so the floor never binds inside the range). The eared case is the one the
first measurement got WRONG: segment REVERSAL alone put its fold at w/2, and the gate's K4 found
the inner edge still crossing itself at that floor — the ear's last offset face runs back past its
first for every sz < w. The tool counts crossings as well as reversals since.

**K5** in `verify-frame.mjs` holds it: at each floor (rounded, eared, notched, oval narrow and
wide, two band widths) nothing is told, nothing folds, and the inner edge's least feature — a
triple's circumradius on a curve, a segment or a clearance on a step — is the promised one
(0.5000 mm of inner radius; √2·F across the ear's opened loop; the notch's 2 mm); just past the
floor the corner is told and built AT it (the same SVG string). Mutants: each clamp removed
(`the-rounded-clamp-removed`, `the-oval-clamp-removed`), the notch floor back at phase 2b's guess,
the ear held only to its reversal.

**Bytes:** the only geometry change is the notched floor, which binds below 19 mm at the default

### 10.2 The scroll primitive (`frame-filigree.js`)

**One law, by curvature.** From the scroll's MIDDLE (its root: the back of a C, the inflection of
an S) outward, each half's radius of curvature runs LINEARLY in arc length from the body's
`ρ_mid = SCROLL_BODY · length` (C 0.42, S 0.60) down to the volute's start `ρ_v` over the half's
first half, then linearly from `ρ_v` to the EYE over its last `VOLUTE_SHARE` (0.5) — a log
spiral, the curve whose radius of curvature is linear in arc length. Continuous everywhere,
tightening all the way out. The S's curvature passes through zero over a smoothstep of the first
fifth of its shorter half. `balance` shortens the first half (0.6 = the rib-order ratio).

**Tightness is the volute's TURNS** (0.50 → 1.75), and `ρ_v` is SOLVED per half so the volute
turns that much down to the eye. The first law tried — one log spiral over the whole half,
tightness as its constant k — tied the body's radius to the volutes': a tight scroll closed into
a ring. Measured, not argued: the sheet's first render.

**The eye is the floor, and the turns are what the gap allows.** The eye radius is `(t + F)/2`
— the corners' own law: the wire's inner edge keeps half a feature of radius. The turns are
then LOWERED by bisection until the least SELF-GAP — centreline distance, less the wire, between
two stations the curve turns at least π between (across the eye, across adjacent coils, across
a closing mouth) — is at least F: a closed gap prints as a solid web. Held turns are told. At
the vocabulary's 60 mm / 1.6 mm, tightness above about 0.3 WAS held at 0.84 turns — dead travel
on the slider. **Fixed by Eva's ruling 2 on step 1 — §10.4.**

**Sampling** is the arc law at 3° of turning (half the band's resolution: a wire is a few mm
across), capped at 1 mm along a straight stretch, each step an exact circular arc at the step's
mid curvature. **The wire** is `bandSolid` on the curve as a spine: ROUND (a semicircle crown of
radius t/2 on a wall of t/2 — the upper half of a round wire) or HALF-ROUND (the semicircle
alone), standing on a base (1.0 mm on the sheet; step 2 sets it to the host). Wire floored at F,
told. Cost: **5,500–12,100 triangles a scroll** at 8 profile samples (30–120 mm), so a frame with
40 scrolls is ~0.3–0.5 M — estimated now, for step 2's density ceiling.

**Groups (2–5).** A LEAD C and n − 1 children budding from its BACK at stations ±0.18, ±0.32 of
its length from its root, each child the other kind (S, C, …) at 0.6 × the length per order and
√0.6 × the wire, laid along the lead's tangent and flipped so its own back kisses the lead's, its
root `(t_lead + t_child)/2 − EMBED_MM` off the lead's centreline: the two closed shells OVERLAP by
the embed (never a graze). Each junction prints as a WEB over the stretch where the two wires are
closer than t + F — 7.9 to 16.7 mm on the sheet — and `pairGap` requires no second near-contact
outside it. Mirror is exact (x negated, to the bit).

**SC1–SC6** (`verify-frame-relief.mjs`, over 54 scrolls and 8 groups): closed and one region;
the eye and the drawn turns against the law (the eye extrapolated from the end triples within two
steps of the spiral's own slope, restated from the module constants and the spec); the self-gap
restated off the emitted chords (least 1.0000 mm); the wire floor told; mirror to the bit; every
child overlapping its parent by the embed and no other near-contact. Mutants: the gap clamp
removed (SC3), the eye under the floor (SC2), a child laid off its parent (SC6).

### 11.1 The tapered wire

**The law** (`scrollCurve`, `taperTip`): the wire is its full width `t` through the root and the
body and thins LINEARLY in arc length over each volute (its last 0.5 of the half) to
`max(F, TAPER_TIP · t)` at the eye, `TAPER_TIP` 0.5 — so the default 1.6 mm wire ends at
1.00 mm, a 2.4 mm one at 1.20, and a 1.0 mm wire has no room to taper (built constant, told).
The eye's floor reads the LOCAL width there: `(tip + F)/2`. The curve carries `w[i]`, its local
width per station.

**One producer, extended, not a second one.** A tapered spine declares `widthScale[i]`, and
`offsetCurve` is the place it is read: the offset at vertex i is `d · widthScale[i]`, so the
wire's two edges, its crease and hatch lines, the relief grid and the walls narrow together;
`bandSolid` reads `depthScale[i]` for the crown. Both are absent on every other spine and the
old expressions run by branch — **462 rows (all skeletons × presets × interiors × corner /
aspect / sill variants, with and without every ornament control set) are byte-identical in SVG
and STL to `1c797ea` with the filigree off.** The crown thins with the width but never under
`min(F, the body's crown)` (`crownScale`): at the default 1.25 relief the lead's crown is 1.000
everywhere, tapered or not.

**Every clamp reads the local width.** `selfGap` (the coil clamp) subtracts the two stations'
local half widths; `pairGap`, `joinsCleanly`, `wireClear`, the band contacts and the seat read
`wAt(c, i)`, taking a segment's WIDER end (the stricter reading). The crest's axis slide is
followed by a clearance pass on that measure: a tapered edge between two stations can stand
nearer the axis than its vertices (measured: 0.998 mm until the pass). A constant wire computes
exactly what it did.

### 12.1 The texture model (the law, stated before the code)

- **Real displacement, in the STL.** Texture moves the molding's TOP SURFACE — the relief grid
  `bandSolid` lifts over the back — along the surface's own normal. No painted map, no normal map,
  nothing in the 3D view that the STL does not carry. The walls and the flat back are not
  displaced; a wall's top edge follows the displaced top grid, so every band stays one closed
  shell by construction (the same quads, the same closing strip, the same caps).
- **Seamless because it is evaluated in 3-D space.** The height `h(P)` is a function of the
  UNDISPLACED top-grid point `P` in print millimetres (x, y and z) — world-space value noise and
  cell (Worley) patterns, built from `Math.imul`, `floor`, products and `sqrt` only — never of
  arc length along the sweep. The oval's closing seam, the rectangle's mitres and the arch's apex
  mitre therefore show nothing: the two sides of a join are the same points of space. A grid
  entry that `offsetCurve` trims past a join's bisector is the join's own mitre point and takes
  the join row's normal, so coincident points move together.
- **NOT MIRRORED — the stated exception.** Ornament mirrors exactly, to the bit (§10.5); texture
  does not. It is a function of `x`, not `|x|`: a stone does not weather symmetrically.
- ~~**The direction is the surface normal at the TEXTURE'S scale**, smoothed over a quarter of
  the scale, its in-plane part FADED to zero at the walls so a wall stays vertical.~~
  **SUPERSEDED BY MEASUREMENT (§12.2):** both the smoothing and the fade fold the grid; the
  direction is the TRUE normal everywhere and a wall LEANS (its bottom, the footprint, never
  moves). Kept struck through so the reversal is legible.
- **Floors (`MIN_FEATURE_MM`, an assumption, invariant 10).** The amplitude is PEAK-TO-VALLEY in
  print mm; asked under the floor it is built AT the floor and TOLD (the plate's rule — the
  output is capped, the input never narrowed). No displacement may bring the top surface nearer
  the back than the floor: an inward displacement is clamped LOCALLY to `(z − F) / N_z` and every
  clamped vertex is counted and told. A crack is incised and so is held off the back by the same
  clamp — it can never pierce. A crack's width is at least the floor (a groove narrower than the
  feature fills in).
- **Resolution is refined only where texture is on, from the texture's scale** (a length derived
  from a length, invariant 5): the spine is densified by inserting vertices that lerp the
  neighbouring vertices' points AND normals — so `offsetCurve` of the densified spine is the
  exact same ruled surface subdivided, and stays the ONE producer of every grid point — until no
  band edge segment is longer than the step; the profile's columns are subdivided likewise. Off,
  `bandSolid` takes the old path by branch and every row is byte-identical.
- **The SVG is unchanged this phase** (byte-identical); options for drawing texture are asked at
  the end of step 2.

### 12.2 Step 1 as built — the vocabulary (`frame-texture.js`)

**The four candidates** (`textureHeight`, print mm at an undisplaced point; `scale` the feature
size, `A` the peak-to-valley amplitude):
- **stone grain** — three octaves of world-space value noise, sharpened toward its extremes,
  centred: `±A/2`.
- **weathering** — PITS (one per Worley cell where a hash says so, a smooth dish of a hashed
  radius) and WORN EDGES (the arris eased down within ~0.45 scale of each wall, the wear varying
  along the band by a low noise), with a ±0.05 A grain: `[−0.95 A, +0.05 A]`.
- **cracks** — incised ROUNDED-V grooves along the Worley cell borders, at least the floor wide

  its neighbours) — the F1/F2 bisector distance alone jumps where the second-nearest feature
  changes identity, and a per-border on/off hash adds cliffs; both drew "stairs" on the first
  sheet.

## 13. Phase 3c — the carved primitives: the self-similar stroke, the arris, the leaf, the cluster, the budget

### 13.1 The self-similar stroke (the CARVED wire style, now the default)

`carvedWidth(ρ, cap) = min(cap, max(F, 0.7 ρ))` (`CARVED_K` 0.7, `cap = max(F, 0.1 L)`, F =
`MIN_FEATURE_MM`). The width follows the curve, so a scroll is fat through its body and thins as it
curls into its eye, the way a carved C-scroll is — the constant and tapered wires stay as the other
two values of Wire style. Two properties are by construction and both are gated:

- **It cannot fold**: the half width is at most 0.35 ρ, under the radius of curvature everywhere.
- **The eye is the floor**: a carved scroll's eye is built for a stroke of F, so the tightness range is
  fitted for the carved law (its own key in the fit memo) and keeps phase 3b's no-dead-travel ruling.

The wire-thickness slider is hidden under the carved style (it would move nothing): the stroke's
width is the curve's.

### 13.2 The arris section (P3, now the default carved section)

A crisp spine down the stroke's middle with a shallow hollow either side:
u = |2x − 1|, z = (1 − u)^0.55 · (0.72 + 0.28 cos²(1.6 π u)) · dome^0.25, 16 samples across so the
spine is a vertex. Ornament section offers arris / half-round / round.

### 13.3 The leaf (P4)

A short spine whose curvature rises toward the tip, κ(s) = ±θ (p + 1)/L · (s/L)^p (θ 2.2 rad, p 2.2),
so the whole leaf turns exactly θ and the tip curls over. Its width is a lobed, notched envelope
(4 lobes, `LEAF_ASPECT` 0.38 of its length) held under 1.6 ρ (the leaf's own fold guard, wider than
the stroke's because a leaf is a flat blade, not a coil) and floored at F. Section: a raised midrib on
a flat blade with turned-up edges.

### 13.4 The cluster (P5): leaves lapping a scroll's root, as stacked shells

Each group's lead scroll carries two leaves at its root (`LEAF_LAYOUT`, swept over 256 layouts for
the longest mean leaf: +0.3 rad at 0.42 L, π + 0.2 rad at 0.35 L). A leaf is its own closed shell,
LIFTED by its host's crown at the root (`leafLift`), so it laps OVER the scroll as an undercut in
relief while the height field stands. Two contact rules:

- with its HOST, a joint: every approach an overlap of the embed (EMBED_MM);
- with any OTHER wire, clear by a feature, or a CROSSING whose every approach under a feature reaches
  an overlap of F/2 (`LEAF_CROSS`) — a leaf lying over a child is held there, never a near miss.

A leaf that fits neither way is shortened by 15 % up to seven times, then dropped; both are told.

0.6, tightness 1, every group) on a 600 mm print the relief is 831,272 (rect) / 1,152,588 (arch) /
876,744 (oval) triangles — crowding gives groups way first. TB2 therefore exercises the give-way by

## 14. Phase 3d — running chains, seated on the molding

### 14.0 Rulings (Eva, Oct 28, on the filigree critique sheet)

- **The ornament must FOLLOW THE FRAME.** The phase-3a pairs read as floating, not incorporated.
  Target (b): references 2 and 3, and reference 4's corner runs are the same idea.
- **2B, SEATED:** the ornament's root stands on the molding's own height field, and its top clears the
  molding top.
- **4B, RUNNING CHAINS:** C–S–C scroll-and-leaf chains whose centreline derives from the band's path
  via `offsetCurve`, sized to the run between anchors, meeting at the crest, the corners or springings
  and the bottom as junctions.
- **No visible gap between ornament and band anywhere. A motif on the axis.**

The chains are the new default layout (`ornLayout` chains / groups). The groups stay one choice away,
and every row of theirs builds as it did.

### 14.1 The runs (`CHAIN_RUNS`, `runPath`)

**A RUN is the band between two consecutive right-hand anchors**, read as a path:
- The path is `offsetCurve(spine, −w/2 + δ)`, at δ = `CHAIN_SEAT` (0.3) × the molding width inside
  the band's OUTER edge. `offsetCurve` is the one producer of every parallel curve, so a chain cannot
  drift from the band.
- It is resampled at 0.5 mm of arc and smoothed by 24 Laplacian passes.
- **An anchor ON the axis cuts the path where it crosses x = 0**, so the two mirrored halves meet
  exactly.
- **An anchor on the same spine (a rectangle's corner) cuts at its full offset vertex**, so a run
  reaches the mitre rather than stopping short of it.

The runs, each switched by the control it replaces:

| skeleton | run | anchors | switched by |
|---|---|---|---|
| arch | head | apex → right springing | Crest |
| arch | jamb | right springing → sill | Sides |
| rectangle | top | top centre → top-right corner | Crest |
| rectangle | side | top-right → bottom-right corner | Sides |
| rectangle | bottom | bottom-right corner → bottom centre | Bottom centre |
| oval | upper | top → right side | Crest |
| oval | lower | right side → bottom | Bottom centre |

An arch has no bottom run, so Bottom centre hides on an arch under the chains. A run whose anchor the
frame does not carry (an arch with no sill) is not built, told.

### 14.2 The chain (`buildChain`, `mapToPath`)

**Elements.**
- N elements, a C at each end and S's between, N = max(3, round(L / lead × density / 3)). L is the
  run's length; the lead is `CHAIN_LEAD` (2.5, the scale slider's own default) × the molding width.
- **A chain is sized by its run, so the groups' scale, wire and wire-style controls hide under the
  chains.** The chains are carved, by ruling 1B.
- Each element is laid in the path's frame (x = arc length, y = outward) and mapped through it, and
  its carved widths are re-read off the mapped curve.
- **Elements are laid SEQUENTIALLY on the MAPPED geometry.** A curved run compresses whatever lies
  over the molding, so each element is pushed along until it clears the one before it (and, at the
  axis, its own mirror) by F + `CHAIN_MARGIN` (0.1 mm). A chain that overruns its run has every
  element scaled down and is laid again.
- Each end keeps 0.35 of an element clear for the junction motif.

**Coils.** An element's coils are re-read after mapping, and again at its final seat, because a
curved run can close them. A coil that would close under the feature is built with fewer turns, told.
**The mapped tangents are UNWRAPPED.** `atan2` wraps at ±π, and `selfGap` finds a coil by stations
whose tangents differ by a half turn or more. Wrapped, it missed stations that turned a whole turn:
the coil check was blind exactly where a mapped coil closes. The gate read a coil overlapping itself
by 1.375 mm on the eared rectangle. The standing mutant is `the-mapped-tangent-wraps`.
**Fewer turns cannot undo a fold the PATH makes.** An S laid across an ear's corner, sharper than the
element itself, folded onto itself: stations 0.13 mm apart after turning 3.46 rad. The coil re-check
saw it and could only reduce turns, so the element was built anyway. An element whose coils still
close at its final seat gives way now, told (`a-folded-element-is-built`). **[Fourth round of 3e step 3: that mutant is RETIRED — with the chains capped to the band an element is a third of its lead and folds nowhere: 0 over the gate's rows and 18 eared / notched states, measured (§17.10). The refusal stays as a guard.]**

**No visible gap, and nothing in the opening** (`slitWorst`, the opening test).
- Every stretch of an element within 2F of the band must reach an embed. An approach that comes near
  and does not touch reads as a slit, and the element is pushed onto the molding until it holds.
- **This is read at BOTH 2F and F.** A stretch that leaves the band and curls back is one run at 2F
  and two at F, and the second is a graze the coarser reading alone lets through.
- An element pushed onto the molding stops at its inner edge. The opening is `plateOutline` (its
  owner) less every band's footprint.
- An element that cannot hold both rules is not built, told.

**Leaves.** Two leaves per element lap its root, as phase 3c's stacked shells on the host's crown.

### 14.3 The junctions (the motifs)

- **On the axis, a PALMETTE (ref 3's crest).** A straight centre leaf lies exactly on x = 0, with
  its normal along ∓x so it is its own mirror to the bit. A pair of leaves either side fans 0.6 rad
  off it.
- **At an interior junction (springing, corner, the oval's side), a ROSETTE:** five two-lobed petals
  about the meeting point, gated by Corners / springings.
- **Motif leaves are settled FIRST.** A chain's leaf gives way to a motif, never the reverse, because
  the motif is what marks the junction.

**Settling (`settleChains`).**
- Scroll elements clear every other scroll by F + the margin, or the later one is not built.
- Leaves join their host, and with every other wire are either a feature clear or a crossing (an
  overlap of `LEAF_CROSS`), and obey the band rule above.
- A leaf that fails is remade 15 % shorter, up to seven times, then dropped, told.

### 14.4 SEATED (2B): the molding's own height field

**Every chain row's crown stands on the molding as the STL carries it.**
- `moldingHeightField` builds the frame's bands with the relief's own `bandSolid` into a scratch mesh
  and rasterises their tops, so it is the highest band where bands embed.
- `seatOn` reads that field across each row's whole span (its local width plus a tenth) at 17
  stations.
- The row's `baseLift` is the larger of that and the run's own profile ramp, which carries the seat
  down off the band's edge.
- **The seat is then read AT THE EMITTED COLUMNS** (`seatEmitted`): the wire's own solid is built on
  that first seat, and each row is lifted to the highest molding under its own top vertices. Those
  stand off the centreline's normal by the spine's own offset curves, and a seat read on the
  centreline's span cleared the molding by 0.989 mm where the crown is the 1 mm floor.
- **The seat is read at each vertex AND its mirror image.** The raster is not symmetric to the bit,
  and a one-sided seat broke OR5 on the eared rectangle.
- `bandSolid` stands the crown at plateT + baseLift + z × depth, and its base stays at 0, so the
  ornament and the molding overlap all the way down. There is no underside to show a gap.

**The first cut read the run's own profile in the run's own frame. That frame drifts wherever the
band does not follow the path:**
- 0.06 mm under the molding on a rounded corner, and 0.30 mm on an ear.

### 17.2 The weight field w(s) (`frame-vine.js`)

- **From the skeleton's named anchors, never placed per frame.** Each anchor a run begins or ends at

  1 − smoothstep(d / (0.55 L)), floored at 0.15 — highest at the crest, secondary peaks at the corners /
  springings / sides / bottom, lowest mid-run.

  w_h = (1 − h)·½ + h·w. **h = 0 is not the vine at all**: phase 3d's chains run, by branch.

  its meander amplitude (0.12 + 0.2 w) M (held on the band), its half wavelength — one item per half wave —

### 18.1 The construction (`frame-rinceau.js`, read by `vineFor`)

- **The stem** is the vine's (one per run, y = y_c + A sin θ, θ' = π / P), with the rinceau's own laws: amplitude
  `RIN.A` [0.30, 0.10] × M (capped by the band, so it swings near both edges), half wavelength `RIN.P`
  [1.15, 0.55] × M × the WAVE factor (`RIN_WAVE`: loose 1.4 / medium 1.0 / tight 0.72), width `RIN.stemW`
  [0.12, 0.05] × M (thinner than the vine's: the volutes carry the weight). Every extremum gets a volute (the
  zero crossings nothing), so they alternate sides by construction.
- **The tendril** (`rinceauTendril`) branches `RIN.branch` 0.18 of the half wave past the extremum, on the stem's
  descent, leaving the stem's tangent by only `RIN.diverge` 0.25 rad (a branch, not a spur), and is the second
  half of a carved C: a SWEEP (radius `RIN.sweep` 1.1 × its length) across the hollow, then a VOLUTE
  (`RIN.share` 0.62 of its length) winding toward the crest of its own wave. Its length `RIN.len` [1.05, 0.45]
  × M follows w(s) and the compression; its width the carved law under `RIN.wireK` 0.85 of the stem's (a
  branch is thinner than its stem). The asked VOLUTE turns (`RIN_TURNS` few 0.6 / medium 0.95 / many 1.35) are
  lowered by bisection until every coil gap is F + 0.1 (the vine's clearance); the turn DRAWN (root to eye,
  the tendril's own tangent) is what the captions report.
- **Two shapes were tried and refused first (measured on plan dumps):** (1) the vine's own tendril law, a C half
  with a 0.42 L body — tiny ½-turn hooks pinched against the stem; (2) a single spiral whose radius falls
  linearly from the stem — its eye drags back toward its own root on the stem, so every turn past ~0.7 met
  the stem a second time (a graze) and the three turn variants built identically (0.62–0.68 everywhere, even
  on a 16 mm band). The sweep-then-volute is the classical form and the one that lets the volute sit in the
  middle of the hollow.

## 19. STL integrity — one build, no coincident join faces, the print-readiness report (Nov 11)

Read before touching `frame-math.js`, `reliefSpines`, a spine's `embed` / `continues`, `stubInset`, `plateOutline`,
`frame-print.js`, the relief's `roles`, or `tools/verify-frame-stl.mjs`. Both §17.6 open defects are closed here. No look
changed: the SVG is byte-identical on every row, and the STL moves only at the junctions (the census at the end).

### 19.1 One build: the page's STL is Node's (SP)

**The cause was measured, not guessed: it was the engines' Math, not the page's code path.** Built with the same params
(read back off the page's own `getParams()`), the page and Node took the same code path, read no cache, and had no stale
default. What differed was the last bits of `Math.sin`, `cos`, `atan`, `atan2`, `exp`, `log` and `pow`: Chrome 153
against Node 22's V8 12.4, 20,000 random inputs a function, **3–18 % differ**. `Math.hypot` and `Math.sqrt` agree.

Two consequences follow:
- **A last bit becomes a float32 move now and then.** That is the oval's STL, which differed at an equal triangle count.
- **It becomes a discrete decision where the frame rounds a transcendental.** The arch's 1.5° arc ladder takes
  `Math.ceil(angle / 1.5°)`, and an equilateral arch's sub-arch sweeps exactly 60°, a whole 40 steps. `ceil` of 40 and of
  40.000000000000007 are 40 and 41, which is the arch's +384 triangles (two sub-arches, one extra row each), with the
  filigree off.

**The fix makes the page compute Node's doubles by construction.** `frame-math.js` is a line-for-line port of V8's own
fdlibm (`src/base/ieee754.cc` at 12.4.254.21, the code Node runs for `Math.*`). It is written in `+ − × ÷` and `sqrt`,
which are correctly rounded in every engine, plus word access through one `Float64Array`. Every frame geometry module
calls `FM.*`, and `a ** b` became `FM.pow` (that operator IS `Math.pow`).

**The Node build is unchanged.** FM1 compares every function with the running Node's `Math.*` under `Object.is` over
1.4 M inputs a function, in seven ranges including the long reduction path and every exponent, plus the special values.
0 differ, so the 51 rows that hold below hold to the byte. CI's Node 20 (V8 11.3) carries the same fdlibm, and FM1 is
the witness for that too.

**SP, in Chromium:** the SHA-256 of the very bytes Get STL saves (`window.__frame.stlDigest`) equals Node's `exportStl`
SHA over 15 rows:
- all three skeletons at the default, rinceau, dense rinceau and groups;
- a backed stepped-sill cove arch, four mullions with no sill, and circle beading.

**15 of 15 equal**, with equal triangle counts.

**FM0** refuses any transcendental `Math.` call or `**` in the nine geometry modules (comments and strings blanked). A
module missing from that list is not read, and the list IS the coverage.

### 19.2 No coincident join faces (CP1)

**The springing seam was one instance of a class.** Every embedded junction in the old relief ran its full section into
the neighbour. The stub's top and bottom therefore lay in the neighbour's own top and back planes, and its walls on the
neighbour's walls wherever the two shared a section. Measured with CP1's bars, the old default arch had coincident join
faces at:
- the springing (head against jamb);
- every light against its mullion;
- the jamb feet on the sill;
- the backed plate's wall against the band walls.

**The fix is at the boundary owners, three of them, not at call sites.**

1. **A JOIN BETWEEN TWO BANDS OF ONE SECTION THAT CONTINUE INTO EACH OTHER IS NOT A JOIN.** The skeleton declares it:
   `s.continues = { start, end }` replaces `embed` on the head (into both jambs) and on each light (into its mullion).
   `reliefSpines(model)` is the one owner that sweeps such a chain as ONE band. Reversed members have their embeds swapped
   with them. The joint vertex is the earlier member's, and the gate keeps a `members` record so R can hold every chain row
   to its own member's `offsetCurve`. The springing therefore has no face at all.
   - A declared continuation that does not meet its band is recorded (`unmet`) and fails B. It never throws: a geometry
     defect is a red check, never a crash, and `a-detached-mullion` is that check's witness.
2. **AN EMBED IS A NARROWER, SHALLOWER STUB.** `stubInset(bandWidth) = min(EMBED_MM / 10, bandWidth / 8)`. The stub's
   columns are clamped that far inside the band's own outer columns, and its bottom is raised by the same amount, so a stub
   is strictly inside the neighbour in every direction and no face of it lies in a plane of the neighbour's. The stub's
   rows still run from `offsetCurve` (R is unchanged), and the sill's LIP is inset the same way.
3. **THE BACKED PLATE STANDS ON THE BAND CENTRELINES** (`plateOutline(model, w/2, …)`), so its wall is buried inside the
   bands rather than lying on their outer walls. With no sill, the arch's open bottom gets a 0.1-plate chamfer, so the top
   edge is not flush with the jamb feet.

**CP1** applies to every row (80: every profile preset on every skeleton and corner treatment, with and without a stepped
sill; 0 and 4 mullions; lancet and semicircle; backed, recessed and no-sill; small and large print; the rinceau, the
groups, beading and leaves). It asks for no pair of triangles of DIFFERENT shells, one of them a JOIN face, that are
coincident or near-coincident. A join face is a stub strip, a lip, an end cap or a plate wall (`frame-relief.js`
`ROLE`, telemetry that never reaches the STL). Near-coincident means:
- planes within 3°;
- every vertex within 0.05 mm (and within tan 3° of its triangle's own size) of the other's plane;
- an overlap above 1e-6 mm².

The positions read are the float32s the STL writes, and the back plane is NOT excluded. The 3° bar is twice the arc
ladder's step (the old stubs sat 0.45–1.92° off their neighbour's tangent, and the new ones leave at 5.7°). 0.05 mm is
half the finest SLS/MJF layer. **Measured: 0 pairs over 80 rows and 58,160 join faces.**

**What a union would still remove, REPORTED (CP2) and never failed.** The export contract is overlapping closed shells
that the slicer unions. Three classes of coincident pair remain, and they are not joins:
- **BACK.** Every overlapping pair of shells shares the bed plane z = 0. Default arch 21,790 pairs / 152 mm²; backed
  arch 71,779 / 4,142 mm²; rectangle and oval 0.
- **BODY.** Two bands' own surfaces where they overlap at one height. On the default arch this is 22 pairs / 7.2 mm², two
  sub-arches springing from one mullion row. The flat preset's plateaus give 21,814 and the stepped preset's 2,727.
- **WIRE END.** An ornament wire's end cap against another ornament face (a tendril's root under the acanthus that
  sheathes it). The arch rinceau has 28 pairs, 3.69 mm².

A boolean union of the shells is the only thing that removes these three classes, and whether to export one is Eva's
question (§19.5).

### 19.3 The print-readiness report (PR), the three default frames at 180 mm

`frame-print.js` `measurePrint(relief)` is read-only and pure. The page's read-out (beside Get STL: the box, the relief
above the plate, back z, triangles, the file, shells and the assumed-floor flag, plus a **Measure print** button for the
rest) and the gate call the same code.

Measurements are vertical rays on a 0.1 mm grid. Each shell's crossings are paired by parity and the intervals unioned:
the shells overlap, so a sum over shells would count every overlap twice. The plan widths come from an exact Euclidean
distance transform of the footprint, read at its NECK cells (a local minimum of the distance along a ridge). The last
millimetre before a ridge's end is excluded, because a cusp is narrow all the way to its tip: the arch's spandrel cusp read
a 0.1 mm "gap" before that rule.

**What depends on the undecided print process (ruling 3), flagged:**
