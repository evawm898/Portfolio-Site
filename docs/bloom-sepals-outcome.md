# Sepals, part 1 — the whorl (outcome)

**Eva ruled the design; this document is what was built against it and what was measured.**
Read `docs/bloom-charter.md` first. The pointer block in `CLAUDE.md` is the summary; this is
the record. Part 2 (the rim family — lobes, serration, fringe on a sepal) is costed in §10 and
not built.

## 0. The one-line answer to the deliverable

**Instancing is reachable without restructuring the builder, and that is what shipped.**
A sepal is `buildPetalInto` called on a second ring (`fr.sepals.ring`, the hub's rim) with
a second parameter set (`sepalBladeState(state, angle)`), placed by the existing
`buildWhorlInto`. No sepal module, no sepal blade, no sepal profile function.

## 1. The instancing census — how the builder reads its state

Method: a syntactic census of every `state.<name>` read, every `state[...]` read and every
spread inside each top-level function of `bloom-geometry.js` (a scratch tool, run against the
tree before any sepal code existed). The question was whether the petal builder reads a
*module-level* state anywhere, or only the `state` it is handed.

| function | direct `state.<name>` reads | passes state to |
|---|---|---|
| `petalSurface(state, ring, slot, cap, acc)` | 0 — it resolves `ps = petalStateFor(state, ring)` and every law reads `ps` | `petalStateFor`, `widthProfile`, `petalForm`, `spineLaw`… |
| `widthProfile(state, …)` | 12 (`petalBaseTaper`, `petalTipShape`, `petalTipTaper`, `petalTipEnd`, `fringe*`, `lobe*`, `buckleFreq`) — **of its own parameter**, which is `ps` | `buckleIsFlat`, `lobesEligible`, `lobesEngaged` |
| `petalForm(state, …)` | 9 (`petalCup`, `petalCupGradient`, `petalRoll`, `petalRollTaper`, `petalSpineCurl`, `curlBias`, `curlStart`, `petalTwist`, `petalApexSweep`) — of its own parameter | `buckleLaw`, `apexScale`, `curlIsUniform` |
| `bladeStations`, `spineLaw` | 0 — pure functions of the numbers they are handed | — |
| `buildPetalInto(acc, state, ring, slot, cap)` | **1**: `shapeN: state.petalTipShape` (telemetry for A6) | `petalSurface` |

**Every read is of a parameter, none of a global.** The one read that bypasses the resolver
(`shapeN`) reads `buildPetalInto`'s own `state` argument rather than `ps` — on the sepal ring
that argument IS the sepal substate, so it reports the sepal's own tip shape and nothing had
to move. `petalStateFor(state, ring)` returns the identity object where `ring.overrides` is
null (`bloom-geometry.js:1700`), so a spread substate reaches every law through the front door
the per-petal roles already use.

**The resolver introduced: `sepalBladeState(state, angleDeg)`** —
`{ ...state, petalTilt: angle, petalTipEnd: 0, fringeCount: 0, lobeDepth: 0, [petalId]:
Number(state[sepalId]) for every pair of SEPAL_TWINS }`. `petalLength` and `petalWidth` are
scaled through `slot.scale = sepalScale`, which is the whorl primitive's own size ramp — the
inner whorls already take their `layerSize` that way, so a sepal at 0.60 is built by the
same arithmetic as a second whorl at 0.60.

**Cost: 0 bytes on every state without a whorl** (§7: 780 of 845 live rows and 778 of 778
frozen rows hold, bit for bit) and one `Object` spread plus fifteen assignments per build
with one.

## 2. What shipped

Twenty controls, four sections (`Sepals` after `Center`, before `Stem`; `Sepal shape` /
`Sepal form` / `Sepal curl` nested inside it, mirroring `Petal`'s children). Registry total
125 → 145 controls, 30 → 34 sections.

| control | range | default | law |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sepalCount` | 0–40 | **0** (the guard) | clamped to the petal count (§3), told |
| `sepalScale` | 0.20–1.00 of the petal | 0.60 | `slot.scale`; length and width both |
| `sepalPhase` | 0–1 of the petal pitch | 0.50 (interleaved) | 0 aligned · 0.5 interleaved · 1.0 aligned with the NEXT petal |
| `sepalFootBreadth` | 0.25–1.50 of the petal foot | 1.00 | foot = `rings[0].width × scale × breadth`, clamped to `[FOOT_MIN_WIDTH_MM, FOOT_MAX_WIDTH_MM]`, told |
| `sepalAngle` | −90..90°, step 1 | 0 | clamped at the DRAWN limit (§4), told |
| 15 twins | the petal control's own range | the petal control's own default | `SEPAL_TWINS` in `bloom-geometry.js`; the rows are generated by `sepalTwinControls()` in the registry from that table, so a pair cannot drift |

`sepalScale` 0.60 is my choice, not a ruling: it is the size at which the shipped 5-of-8
whorl reads as a calyx rather than a second row of petals on the render sheet, and it is
also the size at which the aligned weld (§5) exists — recorded so the two facts are seen
together.

**Hidden AND inert**: every sub-control at `sepalCount` 0 (SP0 asserts the registry's
`sepalsPresent` and the geometry's `sepalsAbsent` agree per row), and the whole family under
SPHERE (`sepalsEligible = !sphereMode`, both statements). Under SPHERE with sepals asked the
read-out says `UNAVAILABLE under SPHERE: a closed head has no underside ring to place them
on` and SP9 asserts nothing was built.

**Attachment**: the sepal ring is `footRing()`'s FOURTH descriptor kind (`fr.sepals`), at
`hub.radius` on the same `surfaceAt` as the petal rings, with the petal foot's own
`overhang` expression — so a sepal foot is buried in the rim on the same footing as a petal
foot, with no stem required. The geometry did not contradict the ruling: on a flat head the
sepal's foot lies in the slab exactly as a petal's does; on a dome it lies on the cap
(`domeLean` carried by the ring).

**Thickness**: the sheet's, through `acc.floorThickness` — no sepal thickness control. The
whole `Part thickness` section (`sheetThickness`, `tipThinning`, `footDelicacy`) is shared by
declaration and the decoupling sweep (§7) reads that list off the registry.

## 3. The count ceiling — what "the petal count" means per placement

| placement | ceiling | `ceilingOf` (the read-out's words) |
|---|---|---|
| RADIAL / SPIRAL | `n` | "the outer whorl's n petals" |
| FAN | `fanCount` (2 × perSide + the mirror-line petal, derived) | "the fan's k slots" |
| CONTINUOUS | `n` — one turn | "n petals a turn" |
| SPHERE | — | UNAVAILABLE (no underside ring); asserted by SP9 |

Under CONTINUOUS the rim's petals sit at golden-angle azimuths and there is no pitch to
interleave with: the offset is applied against slot 0's azimuth in fractions of `2π/n` and
the read-out says so (`a turn's pitch, 2 pi / n (a golden-angle spiral has no pitch to
interleave with)`). On a FAN the sepals take the fan's own lattice shifted by the phase — the
`count` positions nearest the mirror line — through a new `LIST` placement arm of
`buildWhorlInto` (explicit azimuths), and whether that set is mirror-symmetric is measured and
told rather than assumed (`SEPALS: on a FAN with no mirror-line petal` reads NOT symmetric).

## 4. The angle limit — DRAWN, not analytic, and here is the drawing

**Definition.** Scan the slider's own step (1°) from −90 upward. At each angle the sepal's
LAMINA — the builder's own mid-surface lattice (56 rows × 10 columns), with the foot and the
root-blend rows (`u < ROOT_BLEND_END`) dropped — is tested against every petal's lamina. A
contact is a sepal lattice point whose NEAREST point on any petal lamina is interior, within
one sheet `t`, on the petal's +n side (or coincident); or a sepal lattice segment crossing a
petal facet, in either direction. The limit is the last clear angle; the built angle is
`min(asked, limit)`. **Both modes are drawn and the smaller limit is taken**, so one state
builds one angle in both — which petals a sepal clips is topology and the export floor may
not decide it (session 32's rule, applied a fifth time). The petals in the other mode are
re-evaluated on the same (u, v) lattice through `petalSurface().rowAt(u).sect(v)`, never a
second law.

**Why the root blend is excluded**: measured, an interleaved sepal's margin swings 0.022 mm
over a neighbouring petal's plane 1 mm from the rim, because the two leave a CIRCLE at
different azimuths — root-exit stacking, the crowding instrument's own region, not a clip.
With those rows in, every state read "contact" at the very first angle.

**What it costs**: the sepal lamina is built once per seam-step bucket and RIGIDLY ROTATED
about the rim tangent through the foot base (residual 1e-14 mm against a rebuild), and only
one sepal per CONGRUENT NEIGHBOURHOOD is tested (the petals' azimuths relative to the sepal,
sorted — 5 distinct on the shipped whorl, 1 on an aligned 8-on-8). 226–234 angles per build;
**230–650 ms per build** on the shipped rows (the read-out prints the figure), 0.6 s at 40
on 40. The scan runs on every rebuild — it is the one cost this feature adds to a build with
a whorl and it is told.

**The limits, measured** (the builder's own record; `node tools/bloom-sepal-contact.mjs`
section A reproduces every figure and adds the dense drawing beside it):

| corolla | phase | size | count | limit (live / export) | first contact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat, petals at tilt 25 | 0.5 (interleaved) | 0.60 | 5 | 21 / 21 | 22° sepal 0 CROSSES petal 0 |
| flat | 0 (aligned) | 0.60 | 8 | 24 / 24 | 25° sepal 0 LIES IN petal 0 — the petal tilt less a step |
| flat | 0.5 | 0.60 | 8 | 21 / 21 | crossing |
| cupped 0.6 | 0.5 | 0.60 | 5 | 25 / 25 | 26° sepal 1 stands on top of petal 2 |
| flat, 40 on 40 | 0.5 | 1.00 | 40 | 22 / 22 | — |
| curl 120 at the base (`sepalCurlBias` 0) | 0.5 | 0.60 | 5 | **−37** | the curled tip over the petal |

So the limit MOVES WITH THE PHASE (aligned 24, interleaved 21 on the same corolla), with the
form (a cupped corolla lets an interleaved sepal 4° further), and with the sepal's own curl
(a sepal curling upward is clamped negative). An analytic limit — the petal tilt less a step —
is right for the aligned case only, and only on a flat corolla; the drawn one is what ships.

**Against a denser drawing** (four times the lattice, a quarter of the step, both modes,
4 phases × 3 sizes × 3 counts on a flat and on a cupped corolla — **72 states, 0
disagreements, worst early-contact 0.00°**): the dense drawing finds contact no earlier than
the builder's limit on every state, and its own last clear angle sits 0.00–0.75° ABOVE the
builder's (the builder's step is 1°, the dense one 0.25°, and a coarser lattice can only miss
a contact between its lines, never invent one). The shipped limit is therefore the coarser
of two agreeing drawings, and the residual it carries is the step, not the lattice. Two
things the sweep says beyond agreement: **at size 0.20 no sepal ever meets a petal** (limit
90° at every phase and count, both corollas — a 7 mm sepal clears a 35 mm petal at any
angle, and the read-out says `no contact in the range`); and on the cupped corolla the
interleaved limit rises with count falling (28° at 1 and 8, 25° at 5) because the
neighbourhood a sepal must clear differs — the limit is a property of the STATE, which is
why it is drawn per build. `--control` rotates the dense sepal the wrong way and section A
reports 8 disagreements on the quick grid.

**Beyond the limit** is reachable through the capability hook `{ sepalAngleUnclamped: true }`
only — no control reaches it, SP8 fires on any row that carries it, and the render sheet
uses it for its "beyond" cell.

## 5. Is sepal–petal interpenetration a census failure, or only visual?

**Both, and which one depends on the phase — measured, not argued** (§B of the contact
tool; every block-35 row was also built with `sepalCount` 0 so the attribution is a
subtraction):

* **Interleaved (the default), at every angle up to the limit: the sepals add exactly 0
  within-shell pairs.** Each sepal is its own closed shell; the by-design overlap with the
  petals it sits between is CROSS-shell, which the export contract permits — visual only.
  `shells 14 vs 9 + 5` on the shipped whorl.
* **Aligned, at the shipped size: 2,130 within-shell pairs and 4 of 8 sepals WELDED** to
  their petals. The cause is an EXACT-VERTEX coincidence, not a fold: the sepal foot's column
  products are bit-equal to the petal foot's when `scale × breadth` is a ratio the lattice
  reproduces exactly (0.60 × 1.00 = 3/5 — 44 shared vertices on the rim, measured), so the
  census's exact-position weld reads the two as ONE shell and the blades' overlap becomes a
  within-shell count. At size 1.00 the sepal foot IS the petal foot (8 of 8 welded, 1,120
  pairs). **At size 0.55 — no bit-equal products — the same aligned whorl reads 0 pairs and
  every sepal its own shell.** The weld is a property of the vertex-welded shell definition
  (the STYLE-at-272 / CLEFT class), and it is what the aligned rows declare in
  `SELF_INTERSECTION_XFAIL` by name.

The crowded corner (40 × 40 × size 1 × aligned × 90 asked) reads 25,358 pairs / 1.2624 mm
on all three hub styles identically and with no stem — the weld, forty times over.

## 6. The foot on the hub — tangent continuity on the three styles

`buildSepalsInto` reports, from the plan's own thickness law (`hubJoinThicknessAt`): the
underside's slope at the RIM (`undersideSlopeDeg`), the one-sided shoulder at the blend's
edge (`shoulderDeg`), how far the foot's inner end is buried in the join (`footBuriedMm`),
and whether the blend reaches the rim. **The first run of the contact tool's §C found the
tangent misleading**: GOBLET and CURVED arrive at the rim tangent-FLAT — 0.00° on the record,
and the analytic tangent IS 0 — while the EMITTED underside over the last half millimetre
read 15 / 29 / 57 / 72°, because both laws have unbounded curvature at their edge. So the
record now carries `undersideChordDeg` — the law's chord over `MIN_FEATURE_MM` inside the
rim — the read-out prints it beside the tangent (`analytic; the underside falls X° over the
first 1.00 mm in — the chord a print meets`), and the tool checks it against the emitted hub
triangles (a different owner): worst disagreement **0.505°**, the station's own offset.

Export, 60 × 6 mm stem, 5 sepals; `length 0` is the derived join depth:

| style | amount × length | blend | tangent at the rim | chord over 1 mm | last emitted segment (0.49 mm) | foot buried |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GOBLET | 1 × 0 (today's shape) | stops 1.93 mm short | 0.00° | 0.00° | 0.00° | 0.53 mm |
| GOBLET | 2 × 40 | reaches the rim | 0.00° | 48.74° | 29.01° | 15.82 mm |
| ANGLED | 1 × 0 | stops 1.93 mm short | 0.00° | 0.00° | 0.00° | 0.54 mm |
| ANGLED | 1 × 40 | reaches the rim | **81.45°** | 81.37° | 81.30° | 23.46 mm |
| ANGLED | 2 × 40 | reaches the rim | **85.69°** | 85.68° | 85.67° | 46.96 mm |
| CURVED | 1 × 0 | stops 1.93 mm short | 0.00° | 0.00° | 0.00° | 0.48 mm |
| CURVED | 2 × 40 | reaches the rim | 0.00° | 80.56° | 72.31° | 50.86 mm |

**What ANGLED does instead of a smooth foot**: a cone has no tangent-flat arrival. Where its
blend reaches the rim the underside meets the foot at the cone's own slope — 81° at amount
1, 86° at amount 2 — a hard shoulder under the foot, which is the style's definition ("a
straight cone, hard shoulders", the hub session's own words). The foot is still buried in
the join's material (23–47 mm of thickness under its inner end) and the whorl exports
watertight and one piece there (block 35's ANGLED rows, both gates); it is not smooth and
this document says so rather than smoothing it.

**Where the blend stops short of the rim the annulus is flat, exactly, on the record and on
the mesh** — asserted to 1e-9 on every such corner. On the shipped GOBLET default the blend
stops 1.93 mm inside the rim, so the foot meets a flat underside: tangent-continuous in the
plain sense.

## 10. Part 2 — what instancing makes the rim family cost

The sepal substate zeroes `petalTipEnd`, `fringeCount` and `lobeDepth` today. Giving a sepal
its own rim family is:

* **8 more rows of `SEPAL_TWINS`** (`petalTipEnd`, `fringeCount`, `fringeDepth`, `lobeDepth`,
  `lobeCount`, `lobeCoverage`, `lobeCrestShape`, `lobeNotchShape`) — the registry rows are
  GENERATED from the table, so 8 table rows are 8 controls with no hand-written spec; two
  more nested sections (`Sepal lobes`, `Sepal fringe`) declared in render order.
* **Zero geometry**: `widthProfile` and `trimPanels` read `ps`, so the cut, the demand table
  and the fringe's panel split all arrive through the substate. The `lobesEligible` /
  fringe-wins rule holds per blade.
* **The instruments**: block 35's twin sweep gains 16 rows (with pre-guards — a sepal fringe
  needs a terminal); the L and FR families run on the sepal ring as they do on an inner
  whorl (they index by descriptor). `ALL MAX` is unaffected because the sub-controls are
  hidden at DEFAULTS and stay out of the blanket sweep — but a **`SEPALS: ALL MAX`** row
  would be owed, and at 40 sepals × 10 teeth it is a real number: the fringe costs ~+306k
  triangles per tooth on the 240-petal head, so on 40 sepals of 2,356 triangles the ceiling
  is roughly +100k — reachable under budget, to be measured before it ships.
* **The census**: the fringe's teeth and the lobes' cut interact with the ALIGNED weld (a
  fringed sepal foot is the same foot); the weld rows' figures move and must be re-measured.
