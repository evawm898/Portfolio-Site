AI Textile Gauge Reader

An experimental tool for estimating knitted-textile gauge — wales and courses per inch — from a single photograph. This version (V0) uses only traditional computer vision (local contrast normalization, edge/texture enhancement, 1D signal projection, autocorrelation, and peak detection) — no AI/ML model. It is not linked from the main portfolio; you're seeing it because you have the direct URL.

Review measurement areas

The system proposes several candidate measurement areas, sized from the calibrated scale (roughly 0.75–1 in square) and spread across different parts of the fabric, so the gauge can later be cross-checked across independent regions rather than trusting a single crop. Click a labeled box (on the image or in the list below) to select it, drag its handles to resize, drag its body to move it — hold Shift while dragging to keep it square. Review the boxes, adjust or remove any that land on a bad spot, add your own if you'd rather choose one yourself, then approve.

      Results

      Verify by counting a repeat

      Not sure the numbers above are right? Drag a box around ONE full repeat cell -- corner to the same corner on the diagonally adjacent stitch, so the box's width spans one wale-to-wale repeat and its height spans one course-to-course repeat -- and this counts how many times that exact patch actually repeats across the photo in both directions, directly, by matching real pixels, not inferred from periodicity the way the automatic result above is. An independent second opinion, not a replacement for the numbers above.

      Measurement consistency

      The final gauge above is a cross-region consensus, not a single crop's guess: each approved area was measured independently, and regions that didn't agree with the majority are excluded rather than averaged in. This is that comparison, in brief -- see Developer diagnostics below for each region's own numbers.

      Developer diagnostics

      Internal algorithm detail, kept out of the normal results view on purpose — candidate periods, evidence scores, and loop-center detections are useful while developing the detector, not for reading a gauge measurement.

      Detection Details

      What the detector actually locked onto for each axis: every harmonic period candidate it considered (typically half / full / double the raw edge-signal estimate) and why the final one was picked. A "corrected" reason means the raw estimate looked like it was following a sub-loop feature (e.g. one leg of a V) or a doubled repeat, and loop-center evidence was used to fix it — use "Show loop centers" above to check that visually against the actual knit structure.
